Hello Takeo,

Creators on a content platform I designed for kept asking me the same three questions: am I growing, who reads my posts, and what do I earn? The app only showed a basic read count, so they kept writing without knowing what worked. Most earned $1–2 a month, which made the money question harder to answer honestly.

This dashboard is built for regular creators who post often. It shows how their posts are doing, who reads them, and what to try next. New creators with little data get guidance in place of empty charts.

Use the Creator and State menus above the prototype to see other creators and states.

I designed this using the UI I originally made in Figma for the app. Names, branding and users' personal data are left out under NDA, and all numbers are sample data.

Design decisions

Four choices that shaped the dashboard.

01

Answer first, then the detail

A creator should know how their week went in a few seconds. Four tiles answer that first, the charts below explain why, and any post opens its own detail panel.

In complex data products: status first, detail when someone asks for it.

02

Readers lead, earnings show progress

Most creators earned $1–2 a month, so leading with money would have discouraged them. Readers come first, and earnings sit near the end as progress toward the $10 cap per post, always labeled "Estimated".

In complex data products: small or early numbers need honest framing, like estimated vs final results.

03

Honest small numbers

A 50% jump on 8 reads looks like news but isn't. Below a minimum, the dashboard shows the plain count in grey, folds small groups into "Other", and tells new creators what's still needed, like "18/50 readers to unlock this".

In complex data products: low-volume sites and rare events cause false alarms, so every number needs a minimum sample.

04

AI hints you can check

Each hint says how sure it is (Strong or Early signal) and how many posts and reads it's based on. "Why?" lists the posts behind it and rings them on the Reads chart. Weak patterns never become hints.

In complex data products: people over-trust AI flags, so the evidence should be one click away.
